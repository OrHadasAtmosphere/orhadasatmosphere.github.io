Research

Understanding the atmosphere
through its storms.

Physical mechanisms, individual weather systems, and the climate they create together.

01

Storms & jets

Fundamentals of midlatitude dynamics

I study how baroclinic instability, jet structure, and eddy–mean flow interactions govern storm growth. Following individual storms helps connect their energy conversion and propagation to the time-averaged circulation.

My work explores why stronger jets do not always produce stronger storms, and how a storm’s path through its environment controls its development.

02

Clouds & radiation

Cloud–circulation coupling

Storms organize clouds, and clouds modify the energy budget that sustains atmospheric circulation. I investigate how this coupling shapes planetary albedo, temperature gradients, and the seasonal cycle of storm tracks.

I combine observations, idealized simulations, and theoretical models to understand feedbacks among storms, clouds, and the ocean.

Read: Cloud radiative effects reinforce storm tracks ↗
03

Machine learning & predictability

Separating climate from weather

How much does the climate background tell us about an individual storm? I use machine learning alongside physical diagnostics to distinguish the influence of climatic conditions from synoptic variability.

This approach helps quantify the predictability of storm intensity and trajectories, and clarifies the challenges of attributing individual weather events to climate change.

04

Atmosphere & ocean

Storm-driven near-inertial waves

Extratropical storms transfer energy to the ocean through wind stress. My recent work examines near-inertial wave generation from a storm-centered perspective, linking storm structure, propagation, and life cycle to the ocean’s response.
